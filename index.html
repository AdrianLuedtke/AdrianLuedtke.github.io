About

I am an agricultural and environmental economist. I am a doctoral candidate and research associate at the University of Hohenheim in Stuttgart and a visiting researcher at the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT in Cali, Colombia.

I work on how farmers decide, how agri-environmental policy can be designed around those decisions, and what ecosystem services are worth in economic terms. My doctoral research is supervised by Prof. Dr. Christian Lippert and is part of the doctoral college HABIT, a joint programme of the University of Hohenheim and Nürtingen-Geislingen University.

At the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT, I work with Dr. Stefan Burkart on ecosystem service valuation in tropical cattle systems. A joint project with ICARDA adapts a land-degradation valuation tool to Colombia.

My methods are discrete choice experiments, applied microeconometrics, meta-analysis, and cost-benefit analysis. I collect primary data in Germany and Colombia.

Away from the desk, I like to cook, and I am always looking for the next recipe to try. I recently started running, mostly slowly. On weekends I watch football or MotoGP, usually with a good coffee.

Research

  • Ecosystem service supply at farm level. A framework that relates the supply of an ecosystem service on a farm to its scarcity and to the feasibility of implementation. The application is crop pollination.
  • Agri-environmental contract design. A discrete choice experiment with farmers in Germany on contracts for traditional orchard meadows, with estimates of willingness to accept for contract attributes.
  • Valuation evidence and benefit transfer. Systematic reviews and meta-regression of ecosystem service values in agricultural landscapes, including the reuse of value functions for benefit transfer.
  • Tropical cattle systems and land degradation. Evidence synthesis and a decision framework for valuing ecosystem services in tropical and sub-tropical cattle systems, and the valuation of land degradation in Colombia.
